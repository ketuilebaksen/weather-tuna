I want to explain what that phrase means biologically, because people hear freeze and picture a plant looking a bit sad in the morning.

A living plant is mostly water held inside cells. When the temperature drops far enough, that water freezes. And water expands when it freezes, by roughly nine percent.

So ice forms inside and between the cells, and it physically ruptures the cell walls. The structure that held the plant up and moved water through it is destroyed, mechanically, from the inside.

That's why frost-killed plants go black and limp within a day or two. They aren't cold. They're broken.

And crucially, there is no recovery from it. A plant can come back from drought. It can come back from being stripped by hail. It cannot reassemble a cell wall.
