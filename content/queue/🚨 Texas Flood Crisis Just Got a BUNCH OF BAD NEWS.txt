There are two completely different kinds of flooding, they behave nothing alike, and almost all the confusion in flood coverage comes from treating them as the same thing.

Flash flooding is local and it is fast. Intense rain falls over a specific area, the ground and the drains can't take it fast enough, and the water rises within minutes. Minutes. Not hours.

Flash flooding is tied to the storm standing over you. When that storm moves off, the flash flood threat where you're standing generally eases. That's the kind of flooding people intuitively understand, because you can see the cause out your window.

River flooding is a completely different animal, and it is the one that catches people out.

Think about what a river actually is. It's a drain for an enormous area of land, sometimes thousands of square miles. Rain that fell eighty miles upstream, three days ago, is still on its way to you. It's moving through creeks, into tributaries, into the main channel, and that whole mass of water has to physically travel downstream past every town along the way.
