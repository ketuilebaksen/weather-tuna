Now the mechanism, because it explains the eight degree gap in Colorado and the seventeen degree spread I'm about to show you in Wyoming.

It's called cold air pooling, and it happens on exactly these nights.

Cold air is denser than warm air. Heavier. So on a clear, calm night, when the ground radiates its heat away to space, the air sitting right above the ground cools, gets heavy, and does what heavy things do.

It flows downhill.

It drains off the slopes, follows the terrain, and collects at the lowest point available. A valley floor. A basin. A hollow.

And once it's pooled down there, nothing moves it. There's no wind on a night like this to mix it back up, so it just keeps sitting there, getting colder, all night long.

That's why a valley floor can be eight or ten degrees colder than a hillside a mile away at the same elevation band. It's why the coldest spot in a state is almost always a sheltered basin and not a mountaintop.
